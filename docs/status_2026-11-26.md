# Status 2026-11-26: a high-resolution wrist DiT that does not use its resolution, a scripted grasp test, and a restart of data collection with tags and full-HD wrist video

Follows [status_2026-11-23.md](status_2026-11-23.md). No policy has a success rate. The robot runs of 24–26 November were
deploy tests and were not counted. The one counted test (§5) is a scripted grasp at a point chosen by a person, not a
policy.

## 1. Questions

1. Does a DiT that sees the wrist images at higher resolution use the extra detail to choose where to grasp?
2. Is the remaining grasp error a matter of too little training or of poor generalization?
3. Can the robot grasp at a given point on the shirt, independently of any policy?
4. Is the human data good enough to teach a defined grasp point, and what has to change in collection?

## 2. A high-resolution wrist DiT (23–24 November)

- **Method.** The DiT of 23 November was extended as planned (status 11-23, §9). Its inputs add a 448×252 full wrist view
  and a native-resolution 224 px crop around the jaw, with CLIP features from layers 4, 8 and 12. Attention is biased
  toward the image points that the planned trajectory projects to, and a goal head proposes four grasp targets per arm.
  It was fine-tuned from the 34-D DiT on the same 537 training demonstrations, in bfloat16, up to epoch 35. The rented
  multi-GPU server was lost after that.
- **Check: validation error.** Mean position error over 36 validation anchors × 2 noise seeds (a smaller set than in
  status 11-23, so the numbers cannot be compared with it):
  - lowest at epochs 5, 7, 16 and 24: 48.4, 47.9, 47.6 and 48.5 mm;
  - 50–56 mm at the other checkpoints, e.g. 56.4 mm at epoch 31;
  - about 7 mm on training demonstrations.

  The curve does not improve after epoch 5; it alternates between about 48 and 55 mm.
- **It barely uses the higher resolution.** At epoch 31, feeding a blurred low-resolution substitute instead of the
  native wrist images changed the error from 56.36 to 56.39 mm. The high-resolution wrist path carries only 4–5 % of the
  input gradient.
- **The seam barely moves the action.** Blurring a labelled seam region in the wrist image moved the predicted path by a
  median 0.54 mm, against 0.08 mm for blurring a region of the same size elsewhere. Gradient-times-input maps on the
  fixed camera highlight table edges, the demonstrator's arm and shirt edges, not seams. The operator judged them
  completely off target.
- **A seam detector as a side test.** A small detector trained on 218 labelled scenes found the neckline in 66 % and the
  shoulder seam in 33 % of fixed-camera views where they were visible.

## 3. Grasp moments: generalization, not training length (24–25 November)

- **Event-centred metric.** On 302 jaw closings in 41 validation demonstrations, with the grasp flag off:
  - closings caught within ±200 ms: 48 % (left) and 54 % (right) for the epoch-5 checkpoint;
  - on 300 training anchors: 93 % and 90 %;
  - mean position error on chunks around grasps: 39.5 mm on validation against 7.5 mm on training.

  The model fits its training grasps and does not transfer them. A first version of the metric used different
  thresholds for the human and the predicted closing and gave 40 % / 51 %; the numbers above replace it.
- **Grasp-moment sampling does not help.** A fine-tune drew half of its samples from the approach to each closing and
  the closing itself, removed the grasp flag from the state and doubled the gripper loss. After 1–3 epochs it caught 47–48 % /
  52–53 % of closings, no different from its start. Its fourth epoch was lost to a memory leak in the data loaders,
  which is fixed.
- **What it relies on.** Replacing an arm's own wrist video by another demonstration's lowers that arm's caught closings
  by 15.2 (left) and 21.5 (right) percentage points; replacing the fixed camera changes nothing measurable. A probe that
  adds the recent motion history to the state does not predict closings better (area under the ROC curve 0.904 without
  history, 0.895–0.903 with).

## 4. Robot deploy tests (24–26 November, uncounted)

- The deploy first fed the high-resolution model the low-resolution substitute and put the gripper command into the
  grasp-flag slots of the state, because a marker file was missing in the new checkpoint folders. After both fixes the
  operator remarked that it now grasped properly. This is a remark, not a count.
- On the robot, the wrist-camera grasp classifier scores an empty closed jaw as high as one holding cloth (0.96–0.98
  against 0.98–0.99). The grasp flag is therefore taken from the jaw-open rule of 23 November. The jaw state now comes
  from the measured robot jaw, as the human jaw in training, not from the previous command.
- The high-resolution model needs about 690 ms per plan, too slow for blending successive plans.
- Hardware: power dropouts at the left wrist caused motor faults and repeated jaw-zero resets.

## 5. Robot-to-camera calibration and a scripted grasp test (25 November)

- **Calibration.** The previous camera-to-robot calibration disagreed with the table plane seen by the depth camera by
  8.1° and 140 mm. It was redone with jaw-tip touches on table points located in the depth image. This showed that the
  tool point of the robot model lies 4–5 cm short of the real jaw tip on both arms. The fixed camera moved three times
  during the day, so a check against a reference view and an automatic correction from image features were added.
  Final accuracy: about 3–7 mm (right arm) and about 1 cm (left arm).
- **Scripted grasp ("oracle") test.** A person clicks a point on the shirt in the fixed-camera image; the robot moves
  above it, descends, closes, and lifts. Result: 4 of 9 recorded trials held the cloth for 3 s (right arm 3 of 4, left
  arm 1 of 5). Two further runs have no recorded result and are not counted. In all 5 misses the jaw reached the target
  height beside the cloth. The left jaw's finger shape is suspected but not confirmed.

## 6. Data: a restart with tags and full-HD wrist video (25–26 November)

- **25 November.** 100 usable demonstrations on five shirts. Conversion graded 1 GOLD, 27 SILVER and 72 REJECT, mainly
  because the hand units came within 0.4–0.6 m of the RGB-D camera, where it gives no depth, and because the head and the
  shirt occluded them. Filling tracking gaps with the IMU was worse than straight-line interpolation for gaps of 2–3 s
  (median 75 vs 51 mm and 168 vs 142 mm).
- **Collection changes (26 November):**
  - The fixed camera had in fact recorded at 12.3–12.8 Hz; it now records at 29.9 Hz.
  - The wrist videos are saved at 1920×1080.
  - One 50 mm AprilTag was put back on each hand unit. With the tags in the fusion, the jaw-tip position's median distance
    to the tag measurement fell from 28 to 11 mm (left) and from 40 to 13 mm (right). The tags are also an input to the
    fusion, so this check is partly circular.
  - The user decided that the earlier demonstrations are no longer used for training. 103 tagged demonstrations were
    recorded on 26 November.
- **Splits and next training.** All of 26 November goes to training; validation and test come from 25 November. The
  next fine-tune starts from the epoch-5 checkpoint, uses the full-HD wrist input (672×378 full view and a 336 px crop),
  and samples around grasp moments. It is planned for 25 epochs first and had not started. The plan to label all 942
  closings of 25 November by hand was dropped.

## 7. What is not shown

- No policy success rate. The 4 of 9 in §5 is a scripted grasp at a person's chosen point; it tests the robot side,
  not a policy.
- The high-resolution model's errors are on human demonstrations, on a small anchor set.
- The tag accuracy is measured against the tags themselves.
- The new data have not been trained on yet.
