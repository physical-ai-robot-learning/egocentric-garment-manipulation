# Status 2026-11-27: factor-graph trajectories in use, a lost jaw signal recovered from video, and the first training set from the new collection

*Updated in the evening of 27 November: Section 5 supersedes the trajectory choice of Section 2 and the counts and masking
rule of Section 4.*

Follows [status_2026-11-26.md](status_2026-11-26.md). No policy has a success rate, and no policy was trained or run on the
robot in this period. Everything below is about the data.

## 1. Questions

1. Which trajectory source should the new training data use, per arm?
2. Can a jaw signal that the recorder lost be recovered from the wrist video?
3. How many of the new demonstrations are usable, and how should partly occluded ones be used?

## 2. Trajectories: factor graph, tag offset, and a per-arm choice (26–27 November)

- **No held-out test of the factor graph.** The held-out take planned for testing the GTSAM fusion (status 11-26) was
  later put into training with the rest of 26 November, so the factor graph has been checked only on the take it was
  developed on and on the clicked jaw tips below.
- **Untagged data of 25 November.** Against 36 jaw tips clicked by the operator on one take (median, image pixels): the
  factor graph is better for the right hand (9.3 vs 15.4 px for the previous fusion) and worse for the left (8.3 vs
  4.7 px). These demonstrations now take the left hand from the previous fusion and the right hand from the factor
  graph. They are used only for validation and test.
- **Tag-to-jaw-tip offset.** Jaw-tip positions lay far from the visible jaw although the tags were seen, because the
  offset from the tag to the jaw tip had been derived indirectly from an older take recorded without tags. It was fitted
  directly from 50 jaw tips clicked on one take of 26 November. On that same take (so in-sample), the median / 90th
  percentile distance to the clicks fell from 11.2 / 20.0 to 4.3 / 7.6 px (right) and from 4.4 / 8.5 to 3.2 / 5.4 px
  (left); 1 px is about 2 mm.
- **Per-arm choice.** For each arm of each demonstration, the trajectory closer to the tag measurements was taken, from
  either the previous fusion (re-run with the new offset) or the factor graph. The previous fusion was chosen for 229 of
  256 arms. Because the previous fusion is built to follow the tags, this criterion may favour it. Against the clicked
  tips, the per-arm choice has a better median but a worse 90th percentile than the factor graph alone (left 2.2 / 7.4
  vs 3.2 / 5.4 px, right 3.9 / 10.8 vs 4.3 / 7.6 px).
- **Wrong-arm jumps.** In a few demonstrations the jaw-tip track jumps to the other hand when the hands are close and the
  tag is not visible: the point tracks move to the other hand and the factor graph trusts them. Tag-anchored rejection
  of such point tracks is being added; there are no results yet.

## 3. A lost jaw signal recovered from the wrist video (26 November)

- The right hand unit's jaw servo stopped reporting from the third take of 26 November on (a cable or power fault, fixed
  by re-plugging). 106 of the 128 demonstrations of that day had no right jaw signal; the left side was complete.
- A small image model reads the jaw opening from a crop of the wrist image around the jaw. It was trained on demonstrations
  where the servo worked (the right hand on 25 November, and frames of 26 November before the fault, 31,510 frames). On
  4 held-out demonstrations it reaches a mean absolute error of 0.017 (opening 0–1), and it detects all 17 closings and 17
  openings (F1 1.00 for both, timing 18 / 21 ms). A first version trained on mirrored left-hand frames reached 0.035
  and F1 0.82 for openings. The recovered signal fills the 106 demonstrations.
- The same fault recurred in one take of 27 November; it is recovered the same way.
- **Binary jaw label.** The jaw label is now binary, open when the continuous opening exceeds 0.3, for both hands; the
  continuous value is kept. Compared with the previous hysteresis rule (0.55 / 0.35), 1.8 % (left) and 5.8 % (right) of the
  labels changed; the right hand changed more because the recovered signal spends more time half-open.

## 4. Data and splits

- **26 November.** 128 demonstrations. A grading rule that counts a hand as observed when either the point tracks or its
  tag see it passes 72 (the previous, point-track-only rule passed 47). Of the other 56, the operator reviewed videos and
  kept all 30 demonstrations whose only problem was one hand leaving the view near a grasp, and 5 of 26 rejected ones.
  Training set: **107**. Where a hand was seen by neither tracks nor tag for 0.3 s or more, that arm's position and
  rotation loss is switched off; its jaw label and the other arm are kept.
- **25 November.** Validation 15 and test 15 demonstrations; the rest of that day is not used.
- **27 November.** 53 demonstrations recorded so far (target about 150); not yet converted.
- No policy was trained: the first training was stopped by a missing path in its launcher, and then held back for each
  change to the trajectories above. The plan remains 100 epochs on the new data.

## 5. Later on 27 November: tag-priority factor graph, 234 training demonstrations

- **Wrong-arm jumps removed.** Point tracks are rejected when the two hands' points come within 6 cm of each other, when a
  point is more than 10 cm from its own visible tag and closer to the other hand's tag, or when it is more than 4 cm
  from a first solve. Demonstrations in which the track latched onto the other arm while a tag was visible: 2 before, 0
  after.
- **Tags first.** In the default factor graph since 27 November, visible tags are the strongest factor, point tracks only
  bridge gaps, and the IMU fills rotation and short motions. Tag detections whose rotation is ambiguous (about 40 % of
  all detections), previously discarded, now contribute their position. In one demonstration this shortened the longest
  stretch without a tag for the right hand from 15.1 to 3.8 s.
- **Accuracy on 26 November.** Median distance to the clicked jaw tips: 2.8 → 2.1 px (left) and 4.3 → 2.8 px (right),
  about 4–6 mm; the clicks were also used to fit the tag offset, so this is in-sample. Hiding tags in 2 s windows and
  comparing the solve with the hidden tag, the 90th percentile error fell from 47.5 to 42.1 mm (left) and from 58.2 to
  48.7 mm (right). This hides tag frames within the same demonstrations; it is not a test on a separate take.
- **Gaps.** Masking the point tracks and tags for 1.5 s every 4 s on 3 takes, the motion over 0.8 s windows (9,794
  windows) has a median error of 0.1° in rotation (90th percentile 0.3°) and 18 mm in position (43 mm).
- **Per-arm choice.** With the same criterion as in Section 2, the tag-priority graph is chosen for 206 of 256 arms
  and the previous fusion for 50.
- **Late start fixed.** In some demonstrations the right hand's trajectory began only at its first tag sighting, with the
  frames before filled by that first pose. The solve now starts at the demonstration start; such filled frames are
  masked. In one demonstration the right hand's tag error fell from 94 to 7.7 mm.
- **No gain without tags.** On an untagged take of 26 November the new graph gave the same result as the previous one
  (right hand 9.3 / 59.7 px, median / 90th percentile), so the untagged validation and test data of 25 November are not
  reprocessed; their labels, the right hand especially, are less accurate than the training labels.
- **Fixed camera resolution.** The fixed colour camera had recorded at 848×480 throughout; the recording metadata listed
  the requested 1280×720, which the capture code ignored. From the afternoon of 27 November it records 1920×1080 (tags
  46–52 px wide instead of 16–18 px), with a 960×540 copy for conversion. The policy input is unchanged (the fixed image
  is resized to 384×216). There is no accuracy comparison between the two resolutions yet.
- **Training set.** After review of every demonstration video: 234 training demonstrations (120 of 26 November, 114 of
  27 November; 251 recorded on the two days, 17 excluded); one more take of 7 demonstrations is still being converted.
  Validation and test remain the 15 / 15 of 25 November. The loss-masking rule of Section 4 is replaced: every frame has
  weight 1, and position is masked only where a hand was seen by neither camera nor tag for more than 2 s, and only in
  demonstrations that review had flagged and then kept.
- **Training not started.** A fine-tune of the high-resolution DiT (status 11-26, epoch-5 checkpoint) for 100 epochs on
  this set is being set up; neck and armhole labels were dropped from this run, because the fixed camera does not resolve
  them.

## 6. What is not shown

- No policy result of any kind in this period; no robot runs.
- The factor graph has no test on a separate take (only hidden tag windows within the same demonstrations); the tag-offset accuracy is in-sample; the per-arm choice uses a criterion that
  may favour one source.
- The recovered right jaw signal is a model output, checked on 4 demonstrations.
