# Status 2026-11-18: first robot runs of X-VLA and of a new wrist-primary flow policy; why offline error did not predict the robot

Follows [status_2026-11-16.md](status_2026-11-16.md). No robot result here is counted: no policy has a success rate. All
robot runs of 16–18 November are uncounted observations analysed from their run logs.

## 1. Questions

1. Does the X-VLA checkpoint with the best offline error (16 November) also behave best on the robot?
2. If not, what in the robot input differs from the human demonstrations, and does a policy built around the wrist
   cameras do better?

## 2. X-VLA on the robot (16 November)

- **Observation.** With the 84k and 108k checkpoints of the continuation run, the arms did not do the human's motions:
  the left arm rose about 18 cm and crossed towards the right side, which is what the human *right* hand does at the start
  of a demonstration, and the right arm pulled back instead of moving outward.
- **Check: does it know which wrist camera is which?** On three held-out human samples, swapping the left and right wrist
  images changed the predicted motion by 0, 1 and 0 mm; replacing both wrist images by grey changed it by 62, 47 and 18 mm.
  X-VLA uses the wrist views, but as an unordered pair: it cannot tell which view belongs to which arm. The FLAT-PGRASP
  DiT, probed the same way on a robot frame, changed by 109 / 80 mm (left / right arm) when the wrist images were
  swapped, so it binds each wrist camera to its arm.
- **A false alarm first.** The first runs of the day were driven by an inference server whose weights were read from an
  external disk that had briefly disconnected: on held-out human samples its first predicted step was 160 mm against
  0–6 mm in the data. A fresh process with the same weights gave 1–6 mm. Since then a server is checked on held-out rows
  before every robot session.
- **Answer to question 1: no.** The model with the lowest offline error behaved worst on the robot. Offline error on human
  data cannot show a missing left/right binding, because a model can match human motion on average without it.

## 3. DiT fine-tuned on all data (17 November)

- The DiT of 7 November was fine-tuned for 30 epochs on the full set at the time (the 3–5 November data and the tag-free
  data, 0.3× time-stretch). In the one analysed robot run (91 s) both arms grasped, lifted and lowered about three times,
  closer to human behaviour than X-VLA, but with too much motion (up to 47 cm above the table) and no new grasp after a miss.
- **p_grasp dropped.** On the robot, the wrist-camera grasp classifier gave 0.67 / 0.95 (left / right) for closed jaws
  holding nothing and 1.00 / 0.92 with cloth in the jaw; on the right the empty jaw scored higher. The crops showed that it
  responds to cloth near the jaw, not to a grasp, so p_grasp was removed from all later models.

## 4. Data (17–18 November)

- **Recovery demonstrations.** 50 demonstrations recorded on 17 November deliberately include a failure and its repair (a
  slip, a missed grasp, a one-hand failure). Demonstrations of earlier days were labelled for recovery segments from
  frame sheets: 172 of 438 contained one. These labels were assigned by an automated reviewer from the frame sheets and
  have not been checked by a second labeller.
- **0.5× time-stretch.** All 670 converted demonstrations (71 takes) were rebuilt at 0.5× instead of 0.3×, with the same
  per-demonstration grades and trims; video frames are the nearest recorded frame (no synthesised frames).
- **Splits.** Before training, the user moved selected silver, validation and test demonstrations into training; a moved
  demonstration never returns to validation or test. Final set: train 537 demonstrations (622,937 frames, 177 with a
  recovery), validation 42, test 43. These validation and test sets differ from those of 16 November, so the errors below
  cannot be compared with the X-VLA numbers there.
- One take of 16 November (10 demonstrations) is used for evaluation only. The fixed camera was moved on 16 and 17
  November and re-calibrated each time (median overlay error 54 → 6 px after re-calibration on 16 November).

## 5. Method: a wrist-primary flow policy with a future-wrist head (17–18 November)

- **Inputs.** Both wrist images (previous and current frame) and the arm state are the primary input. The fixed camera
  enters late through a learned gate and is dropped in half of the training samples, so the policy cannot lean on it.
  Each wrist camera has its own place in the input, so left and right stay bound to their arms. The jaw state is masked.
- **Action head.** The DiT of the earlier sections, trained with conditional flow matching instead of diffusion
  (10 integration steps at deploy), 32 rows ahead.
- **Auxiliary head (training only).** A small transformer predicts, by flow matching from the same shared context, the
  wrist-camera features 0.6 s of data time ahead (frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 patch features pooled to 16 tokens per frame). It
  shares only the encoder with the action head; the action prediction cannot see the future (checked by tests). The idea
  follows world-action models: predicting what the wrist will see should make the encoder attend to the scene.
- **Problem found and fixed.** With raw targets the auxiliary head barely trained the encoder (gradient ratio to the action
  loss 0.007), because 72.7 % of the target energy was common to all samples. Standardising the target per token and
  dimension raised the ratio to 0.10–0.23. Even then the head's use of the context is weak: at noise level 0.9 its loss is
  8.4 % higher with shuffled than with matched context, and equal at 0.3.
- An earlier variant that fed a predicted future latent into the action head collapsed in every training form tried and
  was dropped for this design.

## 7. Robot runs (18 November) and what they showed

- Runs with checkpoints from epoch 6.8 to 20.3. Observed: the jaws closed on the cloth already at epoch 6.8, but one arm
  often drifted outward, the arms sometimes crossed, and the right jaw almost never opened.
- **Afternoon drift was the light, not the code.** In the morning the hands moved toward the shirt; later in the day they
  moved backward with every checkpoint. The wrist cameras face a window: their mean brightness fell from about 170–175 to
  107–109 between 13:38 and 17:20, while the fixed camera stayed at about 100. Replaying the day's recorded inputs through
  one fixed checkpoint reproduced the backward trend with time of day, and swapping images versus states between data and
  robot showed that the images carry it. A per-run wrist-image gain, fixed on the first frame toward the morning colour,
  changed the drift of the first plan in a 17:21 run from −23.3 mm to +7.3 mm (morning: +7.6 mm). The room light is now
  switched on for robot runs.
- **The right jaw copies what it sees.** Replacing one wrist image by a data image of an open human jaw raised that arm's
  predicted opening from 0.01 to 0.85 (right) and to 0.86 (left). With the jaw state masked, the policy reads the jaw from
  the wrist image, and the robot jaw (a wide flat wedge) looks closed to it. The robot wrist cameras also sit 11–19° less
  tilted and 25–44 mm closer to the jaw than on the hand units (measured 29 October).
- **Control changes kept.** Inverse kinematics now solves the whole 32-step chunk of each arm at once instead of step by
  step; replaying two runs, the largest target error fell from 53.1 / 86.9 mm to 8.0 / 8.8 mm. Executed chunks now run their
  full 32 steps (before about 21–24), plans are smoothed (direction reversals per plan from about 19 to 2–5), and a gravity
  feed-forward from the robot model carries most of the arm weight from the first control cycle.
- Coordinates were checked and are not the cause: the start pose of the robot and the median of the data agree within
  about 2–3 cm, and in the 537 training demonstrations every hand is on its own side.

## 8. What is not shown

- No success rate for any policy; the robot runs were not counted or scored.
- Offline errors are on human demonstrations. On 16 November the best offline model was the worst on the robot; the new
  offline numbers say nothing yet about task success.
- No ablation of the auxiliary head against the same policy without it.
- The lighting fix is shown offline on the first plan and by one operator remark; the right-hand problem remains.
- Next: brightness and colour augmentation and a jaw mask in training, HandUMI-shaped covers for the robot jaws, robot
  demonstrations by teleoperation for the wrist-view gap, and counted trials.
