# Status 2026-11-23: a 34-dimensional-state DiT with spatial attention, a repaired X-VLA, and what slowed the robot

Follows [status_2026-11-18.md](status_2026-11-18.md). No robot result here is counted: no policy has a success rate. The
only robot runs of 19–23 November were deploy tests on 19 November; none was scored.

## 1. Questions

1. What did the wrist-primary flow policy of 18 November reach, and does its future-wrist head help?
2. Why did the robot scrape the table and move unevenly, and what in the robot setup differs from the data?
3. Once X-VLA can tell its two wrist cameras apart, how does it compare with the DiT on the same held-out anchors?

## 2. The wrist-primary flow policy, final (to 19 November)

- Training stopped at epoch 67 of the planned 150. Mean jaw-tip error over the 32-row chunk, val / test (3,117 / 3,170
  anchors, the sets of 18 November): best 48.7 mm on validation (epoch 56) and 47.9 mm on test (epoch 67), against
  83.1 / 81.7 mm for holding still. Validation was flat at 48.7–49.9 mm from epoch 45 on.
- Masking the fixed camera changed the validation error by 0.1 mm (48.7 → 48.8): the policy runs on its wrist cameras.
- **The future-wrist head mostly predicts an average future.** On the last checkpoint, its loss on 128 validation samples
  was 1.00 with the matched context and 1.15 with shuffled context. The action head never reads this prediction (by
  design, as in the system-1 executor it was adapted from), and no run without the head exists, so its effect on the
  action is not measured. The model is kept as a baseline.
- **On the robot (19 November morning, uncounted).** Checkpoints of epochs 60 and 63 closed the jaws above the cloth:
  the left jaw closed 0–65 mm above the table, often 4–5 cm. In one run the inverse kinematics jumped between joint
  branches when the plan went far outside the data.

## 3. Robot setup (19 November): what differed from the data

All runs of this day used the DiT fine-tuned on 16 November, to debug the robot side; none was scored. The operator's
remarks ("much smoother now", "it doesn't scrape now", later "it scrapes again", "it can't grasp") are the only
judgement of the runs.

- **The robot table sits higher than the data's table.** Measured with the jaw tips, the robot table is 24.8 mm (left)
  and 22.2 mm (right) above the table height in the data frame, so a policy that reaches for the
  human's table height presses into the robot's. The start pose is now lifted by these amounts.
- **A joint offset.** Jaw touches at 11 table points showed the left arm's table readings spread from +10 to −29 mm
  (standard deviation 10.5 mm). Correcting the left shoulder-pitch offset by 4.36° brought the spread to 3.8 mm and both arms to
  the same table height (16.9 / 17.3 mm).
- **The wrist views drive the height.** On robot inputs, replacing both wrist images by human ones changed the left arm's
  height change per chunk from −4.1 mm to +13.4 mm; replacing only the fixed camera image changed nothing. Each arm's own
  wrist camera decides how far it goes down.
- **Execution.** Several defects in how consecutive 32-row chunks were joined were found from run logs and fixed (rows
  dropped at chunk ends, a setting that changed how many rows were inserted instead of when to plan again, chunks
  arriving early overwriting unexecuted rows). Joint targets are now limited to 85 % of the joint range; a range derived
  from the data was too wide to stop twisted poses and 70 % made 43 % of plans miss by more than 2 cm.
- **Hardware.** Two left-wrist motors (wrist flex and wrist yaw) faulted together three times at certain wrist angles;
  a cable is suspected. Jaw encoder zeros drifted after power cycles and were re-zeroed several times.

## 4. Method: two policies trained on the same 0.7× data

Both use the 0.7× time-stretch of the 18 November data (all 71 takes rebuilt; 537 training demonstrations, 445,053
training rows; validation 42, test 43).

- **DiT with a 34-dimensional state.** The state adds, for each arm, the jaw-tip height above that take's table and the
  jaw opening, to the two jaw-tip poses, p_grasp, the right-to-left transform and a validity flag. Initialised from the
  DiT of 16 November (new state columns zero, output unchanged).
- **Spatial attention.** The DiT saw each camera only as one CLIP summary token. Cross-attention to 96 pooled CLIP patch
  tokens was added after three DiT blocks. The first version initialised its gates at zero and its output layer at
  random, so the gates never moved and the adapter never trained (checked: gate exactly 0 at three checkpoints); the
  fixed version starts with a zero output layer and an open gate.
- **X-VLA, repaired.** On 16 November X-VLA could not tell its wrist cameras apart. Each wrist view now gets its own
  learned embedding, and views are dropped during training (fixed camera 15 %, each wrist 10 %). A first run with large
  batch and learning rate learned to ignore the images (replacing or greying them changed the output by 0.0 mm, swapping
  the state by 215 mm); the working recipe first trains with the vision encoder frozen, then adapts four of its blocks
  with low-rank adapters. Initialised from our SoftFold X-VLA checkpoint, 22-dimensional state.

## 5. Check: offline error on the same anchors

Validation: 42 demonstrations, one anchor every 64 frames (574 anchors). Error = mean jaw-tip position error over the
32-row chunk on the moving anchors (where the arm moves more than 30 mm in the chunk); "hold" predicts no motion. These
numbers use a different speed, anchor set and segment than the 18 November table and cannot be compared with it.

| | val, moving |
|---|---|
| hold | 123.6 mm |
| DiT, 34-D state, spatial attention (best checkpoint, 5 integration steps, the deploy setting) | **75.8** |
| X-VLA, wrist embeddings + adapters, best (1 step) | 81.0 |
| X-VLA from the public X-VLA folding checkpoint, full fine-tune (1 step, two checkpoints) | 86.5 / 87.0 |

- The DiT checkpoint is the best of its run; its first evaluation, in the run's own evaluation setting, gave 74.5 mm
  (test 73.5). The table uses one setting for all rows.
- **X-VLA now binds its wrist cameras.** Replacing the left or the right wrist image by one from another demonstration
  raised its error to 96.0 / 99.5 mm, and mostly for the arm of that camera; greying both wrists gave 109.0 mm. Its best
  checkpoint came early: later ones got worse (83.2, 84.2 mm) while the training error kept falling, so training was
  stopped.
- **Integration steps.** For the DiT, 2 / 5 / 10 / 16 steps give 81.4 / 75.8 / 75.9 / 76.3 mm (1 step fails); 5 steps are
  used on the robot (128 ms per plan on the robot computer). For X-VLA, more steps made it worse: with its released
  sampler, on 92 validation anchors, 1 / 2 / 5 / 10 / 20 / 50 steps gave 72.8 / 75.2 / 88.6 / 106.0 / 128.5 / 156.0 mm and the
  motion amplitude grew from 0.83 to 1.37 of the truth. A deterministic sampler converges (89.3 mm at 50 steps) but is
  still worse than one step. An audit of the training step found no error, so X-VLA is used with one step.
- **A camera session absent from training.** X-VLA's error there is 103.2 mm, and greying the fixed camera improves it to
  95.3 mm: it misreads a fixed-camera view it has not seen. The fixed camera was moved several times in November.
- **Not like-for-like.** The X-VLA input differs from the DiT's: 22- instead of 34-dimensional state, one observation frame
  instead of two, no crop augmentation, and a task slot that started untrained. The DiT is the robot candidate; X-VLA is
  kept for comparison.

## 6. Data and deploy (20–23 November)

- A pedal-triggered recording mode removes the fixed holds at the start and end of each demonstration. One take of
  20 demonstrations (20 November): train 14, validation 1, silver 2, reject 2, kept as a separate set for now. The fixed
  camera was moved twice that day and re-calibrated each time.
- Before the next robot run (23 November) the deploy was found to drop the spatial-attention weights of the new DiT when
  loading it (fixed) and not yet to build the 34-dimensional state on the robot (being added). No robot run was made.

## 7. What is not shown

- No success rate; the robot runs of 19 November were deploy tests, judged only by remarks.
- The new DiT has not run on the robot yet.
- Offline errors are on human demonstrations; on 16 November the best offline model was the worst on the robot.
- The robot's wrist views still differ from the human ones in light and jaw shape (18 November); the new data do not
  change that.
